# Brief interview: turn by turn

Use this when someone wants an iso-pixel scene but hasn't described it fully, or asks for help
describing what they want. The goal is a complete, vivid brief (template at the end) built
through a short, friendly conversation. The person shouldn't need to know any pixel-art or
programming vocabulary.

Skip the interview when the request is already detailed (it names the place, the characters and
the mood, and lists animations). Go straight to building, and only ask about genuine gaps.

## How to run it

- **One topic per turn, at most 3 questions.** Each question offers 3-5 concrete options plus
  "something else" and "you choose". Lead with your recommended option, labelled as such.
- If an ask-question tool with clickable options is available (for example `AskUserQuestion`),
  use it. Otherwise write the options as a short lettered list the person can answer with a letter.
- **Suggest, don't interrogate.** Derive options from what they already said: a bookshop
  suggests a reading nook, a cat on a ladder, rain on tall windows. Concrete suggestions are
  easier to react to than open questions.
- **Accept short answers.** "b", "yes", "you pick" and "surprise me" are all fine. Fill gaps with
  defaults that fit the mood they chose, and say what you picked.
- **Show a running summary** after turns 3 and 6: a few lines of what's settled so far, so they
  can correct it early.
- **Let them jump ahead at any time.** If they say "just build it", fill every remaining section
  with fitting defaults, show the brief, and start.
- **Quick mode**: if they want it fast, ask only turns 1, 3 and 5 (place, characters, mood) in one
  or two messages, then fill in the rest.
- **Vibe references**: games, films or artists they love are useful for mood. Translate them into
  qualities (palette, light, detail density, pace) in the brief. Don't copy a specific work's
  characters, art, names or levels.
- Number the turns for yourself, not out loud. Keep each message short and warm.

## The turns

### Turn 1: What it's for and where it lives
Ask:
- What will this be? (a: background loop for a lofi or music video *(recommended)* · b: stream
  backdrop · c: desktop or wallpaper scene to leave running · d: a gift · e: something else)
- How long will it play? (a: hours, so it should loop seamlessly · b: a few minutes · c: just a
  moment or a still)
- Where will people watch it? (a: full-screen 1080p video · b: a web page or shared link ·
  c: a phone · d: all of these)

This decides loop length (default 120 s), resolution (640×360 native shown at 1920×1080),
on-screen controls and delivery (HTML file, shareable page, or both).

### Turn 2: The place
Ask:
- What's the main place? Suggest 4 that fit turn 1, for example: a cutaway house · a café or
  bakery · a bookshop · a treehouse · a greenhouse · a lighthouse · a houseboat · a train
  carriage · a tiny island village · a space-station cabin.
- When and in what weather? (a: rainy night *(recommended for cozy)* · b: snowy evening ·
  c: golden autumn afternoon · d: misty spring morning · e: starry clear night)
- What's around it? (a: floating on a little island in the sky · b: on a quiet street ·
  c: in a garden or forest · d: by the sea · e: just a background colour)

### Turn 3: The spaces
Based on the place, propose 3-4 areas (rooms, floors, zones), each with a one-line feel and
4-6 signature objects, and ask them to keep, swap or add. For example, for a house: a living
room (big sofa, fireplace, bookshelves, rug, lamp, dog bed), a kitchen (cabinets, kettle, little
table, hanging pans, pendant lights), a child's bedroom (quilt, plush toys, desk, fairy lights,
window seat), an attic nook (boxes, old toys, a round window, a secret).

Ask:
- Which areas, and anything that must be in them?
- Any favourite details from real life to include (a specific mug, a family pet's bed, a plant)?

Running summary after this turn.

### Turn 4: The characters
Ask:
- Who lives here? (0-4 characters: people, pets, little creatures.) Offer combinations that fit,
  such as a child, a dog and a cat · a grandparent and a cat · a shopkeeper and a bird · just
  animals · nobody (the place itself is the star).
- For each one: age or kind, what they wear or what colour they are, and one thing that makes
  them unmistakable (messy hair and big slippers, a dachshund's long body, a cat's white socks).
  Offer a suggested look they can accept.
- What do they like doing? Offer 6-8 activities per character and let them pick (read, nap,
  stretch, watch the rain, cook, play, groom, follow someone around, chase a toy).

### Turn 5: Mood and colours
Ask:
- Three words for how it should feel? Offer sets: cozy · peaceful · nostalgic / dreamy · quiet ·
  magical / playful · warm · busy / melancholy · soft · rainy.
- Which colours? Offer named palettes with a few colour words each:
  - *Rainy night*: amber and honey, cream and peach, dusty rose, sage green, midnight blue, rainy violet *(recommended for cozy)*
  - *Autumn afternoon*: pumpkin, rust, mustard, olive, warm brown, sky blue
  - *Snowy blue hour*: ice blue, lavender, slate, candle gold, pine green
  - *Spring morning*: mint, butter yellow, blush pink, sky blue, fresh green
  - *Neon city night*: teal, magenta, deep navy, sodium orange
- Should the inside glow warm against a cool outside? (usually yes)

### Turn 6: Little stories
Propose 3-4 small moments using their characters and places, and ask them to pick or change.
For example: the dog asks for pets while the child reads; the cat jumps onto the windowsill to
watch the rain; the child makes cocoa and comes back to the sofa; the dog curls up beside the
sleeping cat; someone dozes off.

Ask:
- Which moments, and how often? (a: rare and gentle, 2-4 per loop *(recommended)* · b: lively,
  something always happening)
- Where is everyone at the start (and end) of the loop?

Running summary after this turn.

### Turn 7: Tiny living details
Show a menu grouped by kind and ask them to tick what they love (or "lots of everything"). Aim
for 30+ in the final brief.
- *Weather*: rain streaks, drops sliding down windows, puddle ripples, splashes, drips from the
  roof, snowflakes, fog drifting, distant lightning, clouds drifting, stars twinkling
- *Light*: fireplace flicker, candle flames, lamp glow, fairy lights twinkling, a buzzing street
  lamp, floor reflections, a swinging bulb
- *Air*: steam from a kettle or mug, chimney smoke, embers, dust motes in lamplight
- *Motion*: curtains swaying, plants moving, a clock pendulum, a hanging mobile, pages turning
- *Creatures*: breathing while asleep, tail curls, ear twitches, a fish in a bowl, a mouse
  peeking out, a moth near a bulb, fireflies in a jar
- *Far away*: town lights switching on and off, a car's headlights on a distant road

### Turn 8: Final touches and delivery
Ask:
- Anything to avoid? (too busy, too dark, anything scary, particular colours)
- Any vibe references (games, films, artists) for the mood? (Used for feel only.)
- How do you want it? (a: an HTML file *(recommended)* · b: a shareable page · c: both)

Defaults to state rather than ask: 640×360 shown at 1920×1080 with crisp pixels, 60 fps, a
seamless 120 s loop, a seed for reproducible variations, a free mode with endless variations, and
a controls panel with speed and per-effect toggles.

## Finishing

Assemble the brief below, filling every section from their answers and your fitting defaults.
Write it vividly and specifically, as a strong prompt would read. Show it, save it as
`brief.md` next to the project, and ask: "Build this, or change anything first?" Then follow the
SKILL.md workflow using the brief as the spec.

## Brief template

```markdown
# Create a living isometric pixel-art {place}

{One or two sentences: what it is, what it's for, how long it plays, the feeling it should give.}

This must be authentic, hand-crafted-looking pixel art created entirely through code: not low-poly
3D, not voxels, not vector art with a pixelation filter.

## Technical foundation
- One self-contained HTML file using JavaScript and the Canvas 2D API; no external assets or libraries.
- Native {640×360} rendered at {1920×1080} with nearest-neighbour scaling; consistent 2:1 isometric projection.
- All graphics (architecture, furniture, characters, effects) generated in code.
- Correct depth sorting and occlusion; a coherent limited palette with warm/cool contrast.

## The {place}
{The setting: time, weather, surroundings, framing.}
{For each area: name, purpose, 4-6 signature objects, its wallpaper/floor/light character.}
Every area should have distinctive surfaces, lighting and small details; no empty rooms or
plain rectangles.

## The characters
{For each character: who they are, look, unmistakable trait, personality, 5-8 activities.}
Each needs several animation states and direction variants suited to the isometric view.

## The world must feel alive
At least {30} independent micro-animations, including:
{bulleted list from turn 7}

## Small stories and interactions
{The chosen moments, who/where/when; how rare.} Deterministic scheduling from a configurable seed.

## Composition and visual quality
Fixed camera, no scrolling. The {place} is the focal point in a 16:9 frame.
Palette: {named colour families}. {Warm-inside / cool-outside note.} Mood: {three words}.
Avoid: {their avoid list}. Vibe: {qualities drawn from references}.

## Looping and performance
- 60 fps target, cached static layers, no memory growth, everything deterministic.
- Seamless {120}-second loop where character positions, particles and lighting match at the boundary.
- A debug mode with adjustable speed and toggles for each effect.

## Final quality bar
It should look like a screenshot from a beautiful isometric pixel-art game: rich detail,
charming characters, and a {mood} atmosphere someone can watch for hours.
```
